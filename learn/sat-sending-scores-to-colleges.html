SAT: sending scores to colleges

Think of sending scores to colleges as one room in a house you are helping your student build — in SAT, it is a room they will definitely be tested in. Here is what is in the room, and how practice furnishes it.

Scores do not travel automatically — families choose which scores to send, and many tests include a small number of free sends if used within a set window after the test. Some colleges let students self-report scores on the application and only require official reports after admission. That policy can save real money across a long college list. Check each college's testing page rather than assuming: policies on score choice, superscoring, and test-optional admission differ by school and by year. SAT specifics: The SAT is digital and adaptive: each section has two modules, and how a student does in the first module decides whether the second is harder or easier — and how high the score can climb.

The reliable way to build sending scores to colleges is short, frequent practice rather than weekend marathons. Three or four focused sessions a week, each ending with a review of every missed item, beat one long session because the review is where the learning happens. When your student misses a question, the question is not finished — the explanation is the assignment.

Watch for the two classic traps in sending scores to colleges. The first is rushing: most errors at this level are process errors, not knowledge gaps, and a slower, written-out method fixes them. The second is avoidance: students practice what they like and skip what they need, so let the diagnostic, not preference, set the menu.

One concrete step: sit together for fifteen minutes and look at the dashboard view of sending scores to colleges — where the student started, where they are, and what the next module is. Students work harder for goals they can see, and parents who know the map can encourage precisely instead of generally.

One more thing worth knowing about sending scores to colleges: it responds to feedback faster than to volume. A small set reviewed carefully — every miss explained, every method named — will move sending scores to colleges further in two weeks than a mountain of questions answered and forgotten.

Parents often ask how long sending scores to colleges takes to improve. The honest answer is that it depends on the gap the diagnostic finds: a process problem can turn in a fortnight, while a missing foundation takes a season. Either way, the trajectory shows in the growth checks long before it shows in a headline score.
